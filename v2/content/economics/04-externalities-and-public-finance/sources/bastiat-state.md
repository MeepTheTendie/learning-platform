# The state and impossible demands

The state is the great fiction through which everybody endeavours to live at the expense of everybody else.

For, today as formerly, everyone is more or less for profiting by the labours of others. No one dares to avow such a sentiment; he even hides it from himself; and then what does he do? He imagines an intermediary, he addresses himself to the state.

Every class in turn comes to it and says: "You, who can take justifiably and honestly, take from the public, and we will share."

The state has two hands, one to receive and the other to give; in other words, the rough hand and the gentle hand. The activity of the second is necessarily subordinated to the activity of the first.
