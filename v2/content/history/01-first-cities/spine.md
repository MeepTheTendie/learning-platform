# The first cities

**Period:** c. 3500–1750 BCE · **Region:** Mesopotamia (Sumer, Akkad, Babylonia)

## The setting

Between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, farmers used irrigation to grow barley and dates on land that was otherwise dry. Irrigation and flood control required organized labor, and organized labor produced a surplus. By about 3500 BCE, settlements in southern Mesopotamia had grown into cities.

## Chronology

- **c. 3500 BCE** — Uruk becomes a large settlement in southern Mesopotamia, with a walled core, temples, and workshops.
- **c. 3200 BCE** — The earliest known cuneiform tablets appear at Uruk, recording quantities of goods.
- **c. 2900–2350 BCE** — Sumerian city-states — Uruk, Ur, Lagash, Nippur, and others — compete for land and water. Each is centered on a temple and a ruler.
- **c. 2334–2154 BCE** — Sargon of Akkad conquers the Sumerian cities and rules them as one state, the Akkadian Empire.
- **c. 2112–2004 BCE** — The Third Dynasty of Ur reunifies the region; its kings build the ziggurat at Ur.
- **c. 1894 BCE** — A small kingdom at Babylon, on the Euphrates, begins to expand.
- **c. 1792–1750 BCE** — Hammurabi rules Babylon, defeats rival states, and has a code of laws carved on a stone stele.
- **c. 1595 BCE** — Hittite forces sack Babylon; the Old Babylonian kingdom ends.

## Three vantage points

The documents in this unit come from three different places and times: a Babylonian king's laws, a Greek writer describing Babylon centuries later, and a Hebrew tradition about a city in the same land. They do not tell the same story, and the differences are part of the evidence.
